# An Illumination-Aware Network for Camouflaged Object Detection with Bio-Inspired Visual Processing

Camouflaged Object Detection (COD) aims to identify objects that closely blend into the background in terms of color, texture, and shape. However, existing methods still struggle with background complexity, the inherent ambiguity of camouflaged targets, and their low contrast with surrounding regions. Interestingly, owls can locate prey in environments with extremely low illumination, a capability primarily attributed to the unique distribution of photoreceptors in their retina and an efficient hierarchical visual processing mechanism. Motivated by this biological insight, we propose an illumination-aware network (IANet) that systematically simulates the photosensitive enhancement and signal processing mechanisms of the owl’s visual system to address these challenges. Specifically, there are two key characteristics: (i) to simulate the owl’s exceptional light sensitivity and contrast perception, we designed a new HVIC color space, which is embedded in the network as a photosensitive cue to enhance the model’s information extraction capabilities under complex lighting conditions. (ii) we modeled the processing pathway of owl visual signals from the retina to the higher-level nerve center and constructed three core modules: the photoreceptor encoder, which extracts multi-scale light-sensitive features and RGB features; the neural signal processor, which simulates the information integration and screening mechanism of the biological intermediate nerve layer; and the central guidance decoder, which performs top-down attention modulation and precise target positioning. Extensive experiments on four benchmark datasets demonstrate that IANet surpasses 29 state-of-the-art methods, achieving the best results on 13 of 16 evaluation metrics and ranking second on the remaining three. Moreover, IANet exhibits strong cross-domain generalization across concealed crop detection, camouflaged plant detection, salient object detection, and polyp segmentation.
